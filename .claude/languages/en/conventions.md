# English — conventions

Part of the `en` language pack (spec §4.5). Read together with the overlay for
the tag in force (`variants/<tag>.md`), which wins wherever the two differ.
Option values in force (`pack.yaml` explains the order) win over both. Where
this file says "per variant", the overlay decides.

These are house conventions for the author's text. Never apply them to
quotations from sources, which keep their original spelling and punctuation.

## Authority

- Spelling and usage: per variant (Oxford for en-GB, Merriam-Webster for en-US).
- Style questions this pack does not settle: per variant (New Hart's Rules /
  Chicago Manual of Style). If the author's style guide (`bible/style-guide.md`)
  says otherwise, the style guide wins.

## Punctuation

- **Quotation marks:** per variant (single or double first), with the other
  kind for quotations inside quotations. Always typographic (‘ ’ “ ”), never
  straight (' ").
- **Punctuation and closing quotes:** per variant (logical or American).
- **Apostrophe:** typographic ’. Possessive of names ending in -s: *James’s*,
  *Jesus’s*; plural possessive *the Joneses’ house*. No apostrophe in plurals:
  *the 1990s*, *CDs*.
- **Serial comma:** option `oxford_comma`. Whatever the setting, add it where
  a list would otherwise be ambiguous (*my parents, Ayn Rand and God*), and
  flag such cases rather than silently changing them.
- **Dashes:** per variant (spaced en dash – or closed em dash—). Ranges always
  take a closed en dash: *pp. 12–19*, *1914–18*. Never a hyphen for a range.
- **Hyphens:** compound modifiers before a noun take a hyphen
  (*a well-known writer*), not after (*the writer is well known*). No hyphen
  after an *-ly* adverb (*a highly regarded study*).
- **Ellipsis:** the single character … ; in quotations, an omission inside a
  quoted passage is marked … (or […] where the style guide asks for brackets).
- **Colon and semicolon:** lowercase after a colon unless what follows is a
  proper noun, a quotation of a full sentence, or (en-US) two or more sentences.

## Numbers

- Non-fiction: spell out small numbers up to a threshold set per variant;
  figures above it. Fiction: spell out any number that takes one or two words
  (*forty-two*, *three hundred*), except years, times with *a.m./p.m.* and
  addresses. Keep a sentence consistent (*between 8 and 12 people*, not
  *eight and 12*).
- Never start a sentence with a figure: rephrase or spell out.
- Figures always for measurements, percentages, ages in tables, page numbers
  and dates. *Per cent* (en-GB prose) or *percent* (en-US); *%* in tables and
  technical text.
- Thousands separator: comma (*12,500*); four-digit numbers may omit it
  (*2500*) except in tables. Decimal point.
- Centuries in words: *the nineteenth century*, *nineteenth-century novels*.

## Dates and times

- Order and punctuation: per variant.
- Decades without apostrophe: *the 1960s*; abbreviated *the ’60s* (apostrophe
  facing the omitted digits).
- Ranges with en dash: *1939–45*.

## Abbreviations

- Full stops after contractions (*Mr*, *Dr*, *St*): per variant.
- *e.g.*, *i.e.*, *etc.* take full stops in both variants. Comma after them:
  per variant.
- Acronyms in capitals without full stops (*NATO*, *BBC*); spell out at first
  use unless commonly known.

## Typography

- Italics for titles of books, journals, films and long works, for
  non-naturalised foreign words, and for emphasis (sparingly). Titles of
  articles, chapters and poems in quotation marks.
- One space after a full stop.
- Non-breaking space between a number and its unit (*5 km*) and inside
  *p. 12*.
