# en-US overlay

Overlay for American English on the `en` pack (spec §4.5). Wins over the base
files wherever they differ; option values in force win over this file.
Option `ize_spelling` has no effect here: American spelling always uses -ize.

## Authority

Spelling: *Merriam-Webster's Collegiate Dictionary*. Style: *The Chicago
Manual of Style*.

## Spelling

- **-ize** and **-yze**: *organize*, *realize*, *analyze*, *paralyze*. Words
  where -ise is not a suffix keep it: *advertise, advise, comprise,
  compromise, devise, exercise, revise, supervise, surprise, televise*.
- American forms: *color, honor, center, meter, theater* (also *theatre* in
  names), *defense, offense, gray, traveled, canceled, modeling, fulfill,
  enroll, skillful, program, catalog, dialogue* (*dialog* only in computing),
  *aging, judgment, aluminum, mold, plow, skeptical, tire, curb, check,
  maneuver, estrogen*.
- *License* and *practice* are both noun and verb.
- *While*, *among*, *toward* (not *whilst*, *amongst*, *towards*).

## Punctuation

- **Quotation marks:** double first (“ ”), single inside (‘ ’).
- **Order with other punctuation:** periods and commas always go inside the
  closing quotation mark (*She called it “a triumph.”*); colons and
  semicolons outside; question and exclamation marks inside only if they
  belong to the quotation.
- **Dashes:** closed em dash for parenthesis—like this—without spaces.
- **Serial comma:** on by default (`oxford_comma`).
- **After e.g. and i.e.:** comma (*e.g., the second case*).

## Abbreviations

- Full stops after all abbreviated titles: *Mr., Mrs., Ms., Dr., St., Prof.,
  eds., vols.*
- Time: *2:30 p.m.*; *9 a.m.*

## Numbers

- Non-fiction (Chicago): spell out zero to one hundred and round multiples
  (*two thousand*); figures otherwise. Many science fields use the
  one-to-nine rule instead: follow the brief.

## Dates

- *November 9, 2026*, with a comma after the year in running text
  (*On November 9, 2026, the draft was due.*); *Monday, November 9*.
- All-numeric: *11/9/2026* (month first), but prefer words in prose.

## Grammar

- Collective nouns take a singular verb: *the government is divided*.
- *Different from* (formal) or *different than*; not *different to*.
- *Gotten* as past participle of *get* in the sense of obtaining or becoming.
- Mandative subjunctive is standard: *insist that he leave*.
- Strict *that* (restrictive) / *which* (non-restrictive, with commas).
